# Sector Underwriting Note: Food Processing

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 14: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING STANDARDS — FOOD PROCESSING SME**

**14.1 Application and Scope**
1.1 This section applies to all credit facilities extended to Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the Food Processing sector. Underwriters must read this section in conjunction with Master Policy Section 4 (General Underwriting Standards). Where sector provisions conflict with general provisions, this Section 14 shall govern.

**14.2 Working Capital Cycle and Liquidity Assessment**
2.1 The typical working capital cycle for the Food Processing SME sector is established at **45 days**, measured from raw material intake to final cash collection.
2.2 Underwriters must model cash flow projections using a 45-day cycle baseline. For borrowers whose audited financials demonstrate a cycle exceeding 55 days, the underwriting team must require a mandatory 15% cash margin buffer on all revolving working capital lines.
2.3 Inventory aging schedules must be audited semi-annually. Perishable raw materials aged beyond 30 days from intake must be assigned a 0% advance rate for borrowing base calculations.

**14.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor**
3.1 The minimum Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) floor for the Food Processing SME sector is **1.25x**, calculated on a post-tax, post-capex basis over a trailing twelve-month (TTM) period and projected forward for the life of the facility.
3.2 Under no circumstances shall an underwriter approve a facility in this sector with a projected or historical DSCR below 1.25x. Any request for waiver must be escalated directly to the Chief Credit Officer, Arnold Vance.

**14.4 Seasonality Adjustments**
4.1 The Food Processing SME sector exhibits pronounced operational seasonality. The recognized seasonal peak period for this sector runs from **October to January** annually.
4.2 During the seasonal peak (October to January), borrowers are permitted a temporary over-advance on their drawing power up to a maximum of 115% of the sanctioned drawing limit, subject to an independent inventory verification report submitted by a panel valuer approved by Ashva Capital Risk Operations.
4.3 Repayment schedules must be structured to accommodate trough cash flows occurring between April and July. Principal moratoriums of up to 90 days may be granted during the trough period upon written recommendation from Sector Risk Head Brenda Gomez, provided interest is serviced monthly from escrow accounts.

**14.5 Common Causes of Distress**
5.1 Underwriters must specifically evaluate and mitigate the two primary historical causes of default in the Food Processing SME sector:
   a) *Cold Chain Failure and Inventory Spoilage:* Uninterrupted power supply and backup generator redundancy are mandatory prerequisites. Borrowers must maintain comprehensive Consequential Loss of Profits and Spoilage insurance policies noting Ashva Capital as primary loss payee.
   b) *Commodity Price Volatility:* Rapid upward movement in raw agricultural input prices (e.g., grains, dairy, proteins) that cannot be passed downstream due to fixed-price off-take agreements. Underwriters must test gross margin sensitivity to a minimum 18% spike in raw material input costs.

**14.6 Acceptable Collateral Types**
6.1 Eligible collateral for facilities in this sector is restricted to the following asset classes, subject to the stated maximum LTV ratios:
   a) First charge on plant, machinery, and processing equipment (Maximum LTV: 65% of distressed liquidation value).
   b) First hypothecation on book debts and non-perishable inventory (Maximum LTV: 50% of verified net book value).
   c) Collateral residential or commercial real estate belonging to the promoter, unencumbered (Maximum LTV: 70% of current independent valuation).
6.2 Floating charges over perishable inventory are strictly prohibited unless backed by a tripartite collateral management agreement with a bonded warehouse operator approved by Ashva Capital Operations.

**14.7 Portfolio Concentration Limits**
7.1 The maximum aggregate exposure the lender will hold in the Food Processing SME sector is **12 percent** of the total active loan book.
7.2 As of the current reporting cycle, sector exposure stands at **9.8 percent** of the loan book. Underwriters may continue normal intake until total exposure reaches 11.5 percent, at which point an automatic amber alert is triggered, restricting new approvals to Tier-1 rated borrowers only. Upon hitting the 12 percent ceiling, all new sector originations shall cease immediately.

**14.8 Facility Parameters and Tenor Limits**
8.1 The maximum allowable facility tenor for term loans in the Food Processing SME sector is **60 months** (inclusive of any permitted moratorium period). Revolving credit facilities shall be subject to annual review and renewal, not exceeding 12 months per tranche.
