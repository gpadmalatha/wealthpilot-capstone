# Sector Underwriting Note: Textiles

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 14: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING PROTOCOLS**
**SUB-SECTION 14.4: TEXTILE MANUFACTURING AND WHOLESALE (SME)**

---

### 14.4.1 PURPOSE AND APPLICABILITY
This sub-section establishes mandatory underwriting parameters for all small and medium enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the textile sector (SIC Codes 1300–1399). Underwriters deviate from these parameters only upon the written authorization of the Credit Committee and the Chief Risk Officer, as governed by Section 2.1 of this manual.

### 14.4.2 WORKING CAPITAL CYCLE ANALYSIS
1. The typical operating cycle for the textile sector is **90 days**, measured from raw material procurement (cotton, synthetic yarn, grey fabric) to cash collection from finished goods distribution.
2. Underwriters must analyze the applicant’s Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) using the trailing twelve months (TTM) audited financials and the most recent interim management accounts. 
3. Any borrower demonstrating a CCC exceeding 120 days requires a mandatory forensic audit of inventory aging. Inventory aged over 180 days must be assigned a 0% valuation in the borrowing base calculation.

### 14.4.3 DEBT SERVICE COVERAGE RATIO (DSCR) FLOOR
1. Notwithstanding the standard general commercial SME DSCR floor of 1.25x set forth in Section 4.2, the sector-specific DSCR floor for all textile SME exposures is **1.30x**.
2. The DSCR calculation must incorporate peak seasonal debt service obligations and assume fully drawn revolving working capital facilities.

### 14.4.4 SEASONALITY ADJUSTMENTS
1. The textile sector exhibits pronounced revenue concentration during the **August to October** seasonal peak, driven by pre-holiday inventory builds for retail distribution.
2. Underwriters must utilize 12-month rolling cash flow projections rather than annualized quarterly extrapolations to account for this trough-and-peak dynamic.
3. During the off-peak trough (February to April), borrowers are permitted to utilize up to 100% of their sanctioned drawing power, provided that the trailing three-month average cash balance does not fall below 15% of total outstanding short-term debt.

### 14.4.5 PRIMARY CAUSES OF DISTRESS
Underwriters must explicitly address and mitigate the two most common structural causes of default in the textile SME portfolio:
1. **Raw Material Price Volatility:** Unhedged exposure to raw commodity price fluctuations (particularly global cotton and crude-derived synthetic fibers) compressing gross margins by more than 350 basis points over a single fiscal quarter.
2. **Buyer Concentration Risk:** Excessive dependence on top-tier apparel retailers or garment aggregators where a single debtor accounts for more than 25% of total annual receivables.

### 14.4.6 ACCEPTABLE COLLATERAL AND MARGINS
Collateral must be valued in accordance with Ashva Capital Valuation Standards (Document VC-88). Acceptable asset classes and maximum advance rates are:
1. **Commercial Real Estate (Factory/Warehouse):** Maximum advance rate of 65% of forced liquidation value (FLV), backed by a registered primary mortgage.
2. **Plant and Machinery (Looms, Dyeing Units, Finishing Lines):** Maximum advance rate of 50% of FLV, restricted to equipment aged less than 10 years, verified by an independent chartered engineer's valuation certificate.
3. **Current Assets (Inventory and Book Debts):** Maximum advance rate of 75% on eligible raw materials and finished goods, and 60% on book debts under 90 days.

### 14.4.7 PORTFOLIO EXPOSURE LIMITS AND SPECIFIC THRESHOLDS
1. **Sector Concentration Cap:** The maximum aggregate exposure Ashva Capital will hold in the textile sector is **15 percent of the total loan book**. As of the effective date of this manual, current textile sector exposure stands at **13.2 percent**, leaving a residual portfolio headroom of 1.8 percent.
2. **Maximum Tenor:** Term loans extended to textile SMEs shall not exceed a maximum tenor of **60 months**, inclusive of any initial principal moratorium.
3. **Specific Numeric Thresholds Summary:**
   * **DSCR Floor:** Minimum **1.30x** (supersedes the 1.25x general standard).
   * **Working Capital Cycle:** Benchmark **90 days**.
   * **Exposure Cap:** Maximum **15 percent** of the loan book (current actual: **13.2 percent**).
   * **Tenor Limit:** Maximum **60 months**.
   * **Seasonal Peak Period:** **August to October**.
