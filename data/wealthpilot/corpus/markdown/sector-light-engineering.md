# Sector Underwriting Note: Light Engineering

### ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL
**SECTION 14: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING – LIGHT ENGINEERING SME LENDING**
**Document Ref:** ASHVA-CP-SEC-14.2  
**Effective Date:** 01 October 2024  
**Applies To:** All Credit Underwriters, Risk Analysts, and Portfolio Managers  

---

#### 14.1 Sector Definition & Scope
This section governs credit approvals for small and medium enterprises (SMEs) operating in the Light Engineering sector, defined as precision machining, component fabrication, tool and die making, assembly of non-automotive mechanical hardware, and light metal stamping. 

#### 14.2 Working Capital Cycle
14.2.1 The typical working capital cycle for Light Engineering SMEs is **75 days**, measured from raw material purchase (inventory holding) through production and debtor collection, offset by standard supplier credit terms.  
14.2.2 Any applicant whose verified cash conversion cycle exceeds **110 days** over a trailing 12-month average shall be subject to mandatory working capital compression covenants or referred to Credit Committee (Carlyle Vance, Head of Credit).

#### 14.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
14.3.1 Notwithstanding the general Ashva Capital baseline DSCR floor of 1.25x, the sector-specific **DSCR floor for Light Engineering SME lending is 1.35x**.  
14.3.2 Projections used to calculate the 1.35x DSCR must incorporate historical 3-year average capacity utilization rates and verified utility cost escalations.

#### 14.4 Seasonality Adjustments
14.4.1 The Light Engineering sector exhibits a recognized **seasonal peak spanning January to March**, driven by industrial re-tooling schedules and OEM inventory replenishment cycles.  
14.4.2 Underwriters must apply a **15% downward adjustment** to trailing 12-month cash flows when evaluating debt service capacity during the trough months of July to September.  
14.4.3 Seasonal overdraft facilities sanctioned to support the January to March peak must be fully amortized to a zero balance for a minimum of **30 consecutive days** within the subsequent low-demand quarter.

#### 14.5 Primary Causes of Distress
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants against the two most common operational causes of distress in this sector:
1. **Unhedged Raw Material Price Volatility:** Exposure to sudden spot-price spikes in non-ferrous metals (e.g., copper, brass, specialty aluminum) where fixed-price supply contracts with downstream OEMs cannot be renegotiated within **14 business days**.
2. **Machinery Obsolescence and Maintenance Drag:** Unexpected catastrophic failure of legacy CNC or stamping machinery resulting in unbudgeted capital expenditure exceeding **8% of annual EBITDA** and immediate loss of tier-1 supply contracts.

#### 14.6 Collateral and Security Requirements
14.6.1 Acceptable collateral types are restricted to:
* First charge on plant, machinery, and industrial equipment (valued at Forced Sale Value per an independent RICS-certified appraisal).
* First legal charge on commercial or industrial real estate owned by the operating entity or promoter directors.
* Hypothecation of book debts and inventory, subject to an aging analysis where receivables > 90 days are excluded from the borrowing base calculation.
14.6.2 Minimum collateral coverage ratio (Security Cover) must be maintained at **1.40x** of the total facility limit.

#### 14.7 Portfolio Exposure Limits
14.7.1 The maximum aggregate exposure Ashva Capital will hold in the Light Engineering sector is **14.0 percent** of the total active loan book.  
14.7.2 As of the effective date of this manual, the current portfolio exposure stands at **11.5 percent**. New sanction approvals that would cause total sector exposure to breach 12.5 percent require sign-off from the Chief Risk Officer (Marcus Thorne).

#### 14.8 Facility Parameters
14.8.1 The **maximum tenor** permissible for term loans extended under this sector policy is **72 months**, inclusive of any initial principal moratorium.  
14.8.2 Principal moratoriums shall not exceed **6 months** and are restricted exclusively to facilities financing the acquisition of new, high-efficiency production machinery.
