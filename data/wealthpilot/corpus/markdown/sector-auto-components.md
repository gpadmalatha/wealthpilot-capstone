# Sector Underwriting Note: Auto Components

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 14: SECTOR UNDERWRITING POLICY – AUTO COMPONENTS SME LENDING**

---

### 14.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy governs the credit evaluation, risk grading, structural limits, and collateral requirements for Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the domestic and export-oriented automotive components manufacturing sector. All underwriters must strictly enforce the quantitative thresholds and qualitative constraints detailed herein. Exceptions require prior written approval from the Head of Credit Risk and the Chief Risk Officer.

---

### 14.2 Sector Characteristics and Working Capital Cycle
1. **Operating Cycle Dynamics:** The auto components SME sector is characterised by extended receivable ledgers driven by Tier-1 OEM payment terms and inventory holding requirements for serialized and non-serialized parts.
2. **Typical Working Capital Cycle:** The baseline working capital cycle for this sector is **80 days**, calculated as Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) plus Days Inventory Held (DIH) minus Days Payables Outstanding (DPO). 
   - *Underwriting Action:* Any borrower whose trailing twelve-month (TTM) working capital cycle exceeds 110 days must be subjected to a detailed aging analysis. Facilities shall not be sanctioned if the cycle exceeds 130 days without an explicit structural covenant requiring cash flow sweep mechanisms.

---

### 14.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
1. **Sector-Specific DSCR:** Owing to tooling replacement cycles, commodity price volatility in raw steel and aluminum, and OEM volume fluctuations, the standard baseline DSCR of 1.25x is superseded for this sector.
2. **Mandatory Floor:** The minimum acceptable Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) for any auto components SME exposure is **1.35x**, calculated on a post-tax, post-capex basis over the projected repayment period. Facilities with a projected DSCR below 1.35x at the P90 downside stress scenario shall be declined outright by the automated underwriting engine.

---

### 14.4 Seasonality Adjustments and Peak Working Capital
1. **Seasonal Peak Identification:** The primary production and billing cycle for the sector runs from **July to September**, driven by OEM pre-festive inventory build-up and agricultural machinery production schedules.
2. **Drawing Power Adjustments:** During the seasonal peak period of July to September, drawing limits on revolving working capital facilities may be temporarily increased by up to 15% above the drawing power calculated via the standard drawing power statement, subject to the following conditions:
   - Margin maintenance must not fall below 25% on raw materials and 40% on finished goods.
   - The borrower must demonstrate a 3-year historical revenue realization pattern proving that 40% or more of annual billings occur during this quarter.
   - All seasonal over-drawings must be fully liquidated to the drawing power baseline by no later than November 15th of the same calendar year.

---

### 14.5 Primary Causes of Distress
Underwriters must specifically evaluate and mitigate the two most prevalent failure modes in this sector during the financial statement analysis and plant visit:
1. **OEM Customer Concentration Risk:** Excessive revenue reliance on a single Tier-1 manufacturer or Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM). If a borrower derives more than 40% of its gross annual revenue from a single automotive principal, the maximum facility limit shall be capped at $750,000 equivalent, and mandatory credit insurance or assignment of receivables from the principal must be established.
2. **Obsolete Dedicated Tooling and Capex Mismatch:** The accumulation of dedicated proprietary tooling financed via short-term working capital lines rather than medium-term amortizing debt. Underwriters must verify that capital expenditure for specialized dies, molds, and CNC fixtures is funded exclusively via term loans with tenors matching the model lifecycle, not exceeding **maximum tenor of 60 months**.

---

### 14.6 Acceptable Collateral Types
Collateral coverage must comprise a primary hypothecation charge over current and movable fixed assets, supplemented by tangible real estate security where facility limits exceed $500,000 equivalent. Acceptable collateral types are restricted to:
1. **First Pari-Passu Charge:** On all current assets (inventories of raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods, and book debts/receivables).
2. **First Exclusive or Pari-Passu Charge:** On movable fixed assets, specifically CNC machinery, stamping presses, and robotic welding units, subject to a maximum valuation age of 7 years and an independent valuer’s certificate not older than 90 days.
3. **Collateral Real Estate:** Urban or industrial commercial land and buildings, valued by an Ashva Capital-empanelled surveyor, maintaining a minimum collateral coverage ratio (Security Value to Facility Amount) of 1.33x (75% Maximum Loan-to-Value). Personal guarantees of all promoter-directors holding greater than 20% equity stake are mandatory.

---

### 14.7 Portfolio Exposure Limits
1. **Sector Concentration Cap:** Total aggregate credit exposure to the auto components SME sector shall not exceed **13 percent of the loan book**.
2. **Current Portfolio Status:** As of the current reporting cycle, portfolio exposure stands at **12.1 percent** of the total active loan book.
3. **Underwriting Clearance Protocol:** 
   - When the portfolio exposure is between 12.0% and 12.9%, new originations in this sector require joint sign-off by the Senior Credit Manager and the Regional Risk Head.
   - If portfolio exposure breaches the **13 percent** ceiling, the automated decision engine shall automatically suspend the onboarding of any new auto components SME credits, permitting only utilization of existing pre-approved revolving line increases within existing sanctioned limits until natural amortization brings portfolio distribution back below the threshold.
