# Sector Underwriting Note: IT Services

### SECTION 4: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING MANDATE – IT SERVICES SME

#### 4.1 Purpose and Scope
This section establishes mandatory underwriting criteria for small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) borrowers operating in the Information Technology (IT) Services sector. Underwriters must adhere strictly to the quantitative thresholds and qualitative constraints detailed herein. Where this sector policy conflicts with general underwriting guidelines, this Section 4 shall take precedence.

#### 4.2 Working Capital Cycle and Cash Flow Dynamics
1. **Typical Working Capital Cycle:** Underwriters shall model the standard cash conversion cycle for IT Services SME borrowers at **55 days**. This cycle is calculated as Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) plus Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO, typically zero for pure-play service providers) minus Days Payable Outstanding (DPO).
2. **Seasonality Adjustments:** The IT Services sector is designated as having **no marked season**. Underwriters shall not apply seasonal revenue adjustments, historical quarterly surges, or downward cyclical buffers when projecting annualized debt service capacity, unless specific verifiable client contracts dictate otherwise and are approved under Section 4.5.
3. **Accounts Receivable Aging:** Receivables aged over 90 days from the invoice date shall be excluded entirely from the borrowing base calculation. Retention monies withheld by enterprise clients shall not be considered liquid current assets for working capital facility sizing.

#### 4.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) and Financial Covenants
1. **DSCR Floor:** The minimum acceptable DSCR for all term loans and revolving facilities within the IT Services sector is **1.25x**. This ratio must be maintained on a trailing twelve-month (TTM) basis and projected forward for the life of the loan. No exceptions or downward waivers to this floor are permitted under delegated underwriting authority.
2. **Leverage Ceiling:** Total Funded Debt to EBITDA shall not exceed 2.75x at the time of origination.
3. **Minimum Tangible Net Worth (TNW):** Borrowers must maintain a post-disbursement TNW of not less than INR 15,000,000, verified via audited annual financial statements or certified management accounts not older than 60 days.

#### 4.4 Causes of Financial Distress
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants for the two primary drivers of distress in the IT Services sector:
1. **Client Concentration Risk:** Distress frequently arises from the loss of, or contract renegotiation by, a top-tier client. If a single end-client accounts for greater than 35% of total annual revenues, the underwriter must mandate a contractual debt service reserve account (DSRA) funded to 3 months of principal and interest prior to drawdown.
2. **Wage Inflation and Talent Churn:** Rapid escalation in technical personnel costs combined with bench time between project deployments compresses operating margins. Underwriters must verify that the borrower maintains a minimum gross project margin of 40% and a staff utilization rate of not less than 75% over the preceding two quarters.

#### 4.5 Collateral and Security Requirements
Acceptable collateral types for IT Services SME lending are restricted to the following:
1. **Primary Security:** First-ranking exclusive hypothecation charge over all current assets, including book debts, unbilled revenues, and moveable fixed assets (IT hardware, servers, and office infrastructure).
2. **Collateral Coverage Ratio (CCR):** The realizable value of collateral must provide a minimum coverage of 1.25x against the total credit facility limit.
3. **Collateral Haircuts:** 
   * Book debts (0-90 days): 25% haircut.
   * Unbilled revenues: 50% haircut.
   * IT Hardware and office equipment: 40% liquidation haircut, depreciated on a straight-line basis over 3 years.
4. **Personal Guarantees:** Unconditional and joint personal guarantees of all promoters holding greater than 20% equity stake are mandatory.

#### 4.6 Portfolio Exposure Limits and Facility Tenor
1. **Maximum Sector Exposure:** Aggregate credit exposure to the IT Services sector shall not exceed **16.0%** of Ashva Capital’s total active loan book. As of the effective date of this manual, current sector exposure stands at **11.3%**, leaving headroom for selective origination governed by Credit Committee sign-off.
2. **Maximum Tenor:** The maximum allowable repayment tenor for any term facility extended under this sector policy is **48 months**. Revolving working capital lines shall be subject to annual review and mandatory clean-down of at least 15 consecutive days once every 12-month operating cycle.

#### 4.7 Delegated Authority and Exceptions
1. Any deviation from the quantitative thresholds stipulated in this Section 4 (specifically the 1.25x DSCR floor, the 55-day working capital baseline, the 16% exposure cap, or the 48-month tenor limit) requires explicit co-signature from Chief Credit Officer Marcus Vance and Head of Risk Evelyn Thorne.
2. Unapproved underwriting exceptions will result in immediate revocation of individual underwriting delegations and mandatory referral of the originating loan file to the Ashva Capital Internal Audit Committee.
