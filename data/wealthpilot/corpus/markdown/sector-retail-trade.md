# Sector Underwriting Note: Retail Trade

**ASHVA CAPITAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 4.8: RETAIL TRADE SME LENDING**

---

### 4.8.1 Sector Overview & Working Capital Dynamics
1. The Retail Trade SME sector comprises small-to-medium enterprises engaged in the direct-to-consumer sale of goods through physical storefronts or proprietary e-commerce platforms. 
2. Underwriters must evaluate the applicant’s working capital cycle against the sector benchmark of **40 days**. The working capital cycle is calculated as Days Sales of Inventory (DSI) plus Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) minus Days Payable Outstanding (DPO).
3. Any applicant demonstrating a working capital cycle exceeding **65 days** requires mandatory referral to the Credit Committee chaired by Chief Risk Officer Marcus Vance.

### 4.8.2 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) & Financial Covenants
1. The baseline Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) floor for the Retail Trade SME sector is **1.25x**, matching Ashva Capital's institutional standard. No downward adjustments are permitted.
2. The DSCR is calculated on historical EBITDA less unfinanced capital expenditures, taxes, and owner draws, divided by total annual principal and interest obligations.
3. For Tier-1 borrowers maintaining a quick ratio above **1.10x** for the preceding 12 months, the required DSCR floor may be relaxed to **1.15x** solely upon written approval from Head of Underwriting Sarah Jenkins.

### 4.8.3 Seasonality Adjustments & Cash Flow Verification
1. Retail Trade SMEs exhibit pronounced cash flow concentration during the seasonal peak period spanning **October to December**, during which historical monthly revenues typically exceed the annual monthly average by at least **35%**.
2. Underwriters must utilize trailing twelve-month (TTM) cash flow statements rather than annualized quarterly figures to normalize seasonal distortions.
3. During the off-peak trough months of **February to April**, borrowers must maintain a minimum unencumbered liquid cash reserve equal to **1.5x** of the monthly debt service obligation, verified via three consecutive months of bank statements.

### 4.8.4 Primary Causes of Financial Distress
1. **Unmanaged Inventory Obsolescence:** Accumulation of slow-moving or seasonal stock that fails to liquidate, directly impairing liquidity and forcing distress discounting that erodes gross margins below the sector minimum threshold of **28%**.
2. **Fixed Overhead Misalignment:** Failure to right-size store lease obligations and payroll fixed costs during post-peak revenue contractions, resulting in rapid depletion of operating cash reserves.

### 4.8.5 Collateral & Security Requirements
1. Acceptable collateral for Retail Trade SME lending is restricted to the following assets, subject to the designated maximum advance rates:
   - First-ranking General Security Agreement (GSA) over all present and after-acquired property (maximum advance rate of **50%** of net orderly liquidation value of verified inventory).
   - Commercial real estate mortgages held in the operating entity's or principal's name (maximum loan-to-value ratio of **70%** based on an independent appraisal dated within the last **180 days**).
2. Personal guarantees from all beneficial owners holding an equity stake greater than **20%** are mandatory.

### 4.8.6 Portfolio Concentration & Loan Terms
1. Total credit exposure to the Retail Trade SME sector shall not exceed **12%** of Ashva Capital’s aggregate active loan book. As of the current reporting cycle, sector exposure stands at **8.9%**, leaving remaining capacity of **3.1%** for new originations.
2. The maximum allowable loan tenor for any facility under this sector policy is **36 months**.
3. Any loan application that would breach the **12%** portfolio cap must be rejected automatically by the underwriting engine without exception.
