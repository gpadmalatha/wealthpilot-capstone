# Sector Underwriting Note: Pharma Packaging

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**  
**SECTION 14: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING STANDARDS**  
**SUB-SECTION 14.8: PHARMA PACKAGING SME LENDING**

---

### 14.8.1 Scope and Objective
This sub-section establishes the mandatory credit parameters, risk assessment methodology, and covenant structures applicable to all commercial term loans, revolving working capital facilities, and trade finance products extended to Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the Pharmaceutical Packaging sector (NACE Code 21.20 and equivalent SIC classifications). Underwriters deviation from these clauses requires formal sign-off from the Credit Committee chaired by Chief Risk Officer Marcus Vance.

### 14.8.2 Portfolio Exposure Limits
(a) Aggregate credit exposure to the Pharma Packaging sector shall not exceed **8.0 percent** of Ashva Capital’s total active loan book.  
(b) As of current reporting cycle Q3, portfolio utilization stands at **6.4 percent** of the total loan book, leaving a residual capacity of **1.6 percent** for fresh originations.  
(c) Individual borrower exposure within this sector is capped at INR 75,000,000 (Seventy-Five Million Indian Rupees) or 15 percent of Tier-1 capital, whichever is lower.

### 14.8.3 Working Capital Cycle and Cash Flow Dynamics
(a) The typical net working capital cycle for benchmarked operators in this sector is **60 days**, calculated as Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) plus Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) minus Days Payables Outstanding (DPO).  
(b) Underwriters must verify that the borrower's trailing twelve-month (TTM) cash conversion cycle does not exceed **85 days**. Any cycle exceeding 85 days triggers an automatic reduction in drawing power by 15 percent on revolving facilities.  
(c) **Seasonality Adjustments:** The Pharma Packaging sector exhibits **no marked season** (flat demand profile across all four quarters). Consequently, seasonal drawing power spikes are impermissible, and cash flow projections must be modeled on a straight-line monthly average basis without revenue uplift assumptions for specific quarters.

### 14.8.4 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) and Financial Covenants
(a) While the baseline Ashva Capital SME underwriting standard is 1.25x, the sector-specific DSCR floor for Pharma Packaging borrowers is **1.30x** (calculated as Net Operating Income divided by total debt service including principal and interest).  
(b) The minimum Current Ratio shall be maintained at **1.20:1** at all times, tested quarterly via unaudited management accounts within 45 days of quarter-end and audited financials within 180 days of fiscal year-end.  
(c) Leverage Ratio (Total Debt to Tangible Net Worth) shall not exceed **2.50:1**.

### 14.8.5 Maximum Tenor
(a) The maximum allowable loan tenor for term facilities in this sector is **60 months**, inclusive of any initial moratorium period.  
(b) Working capital demand loans and revolving credit lines shall be subject to annual review and re-certification by the assigned Senior Underwriter.

### 14.8.6 Primary Causes of Sector Distress
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants for the two most common causes of distress in the Pharma Packaging SME segment:
1. **Raw Material Price Volatility:** Unhedged fluctuations in polymer resin costs (polypropylene, PVC, high-density polyethylene) and aluminum foil inputs compress gross margins if contracts with pharmaceutical manufacturers lack pass-through clauses.  
   *Mitigant:* Underwriters must sight existing customer supply agreements demonstrating automatic price-revision mechanisms tied to raw material indexation.
2. **Regulatory and Quality Compliance Failures:** Cost-overruns or operational shutdowns resulting from failure to meet stringent pharmaceutical-grade hygiene standards, ISO certifications, or regulatory audits (e.g., USFDA, EMA standards applicable to primary packaging materials) lead to abrupt contract cancellations by anchor pharma clients.  
   *Mitigant:* The borrower must maintain valid, unexpired ISO 15378 certification and present zero critical observations in the most recent client audit logs.

### 14.8.7 Acceptable Collateral Types
Facilities must be secured by a composite security package comprising:
(a) **Primary Security:** First charge by way of hypothecation over all current assets, including raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods (blister packs, glass vials, aluminum strips), and book debts, subject to a minimum asset coverage ratio of **1.33:1** against working capital outstandings.  
(b) **Collateral Security:** First and exclusive mortgage over commercial or industrial land and factory buildings owned by the operating entity or promoter, maintaining a minimum collateral coverage ratio of **1.00:1** against term loan outstandings based on a valuation conducted by an Ashva Capital-empanelled valuer within the preceding 12 months.  
(c) **Personal Guarantees:** Joint and several personal guarantees from all promoters holding greater than 20 percent equity stake in the borrowing entity.
